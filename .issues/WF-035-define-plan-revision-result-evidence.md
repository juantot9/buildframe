## Question

What Handoff-specific result contract may establish one complete, exact, durably captured Plan Revision from Pi direct generation or a Claude native Plan Offer; how must result identity, exact bytes, completeness, provenance, retries, partial output, failure, interruption, and lifecycle evidence remain distinct; and which explicit unavailable or Manual Handoff behavior applies when a route can establish settlement but cannot prove a complete generated Artifact?

## Blocked by

- [Test the blueprint for completeness](./WF-017-test-blueprint-completeness.md)
- [Probe Pi’s Plan Revision capture guarantees](./WF-025-probe-pi-plan-revision-capture.md)

## Resolution comment

A Plan Revision exists only when a versioned **Artifact Result Contract** is satisfied and one Durable Commit preserves the exact Artifact and the evidence establishing that result. The contract is specific to a result-producing Handoff or Harness Offer and declares the accepted native source occurrence and shape, exact selection rule, lossless Plan payload representation, size and representability bounds, required correlation and completeness evidence, and provenance to retain. It sits beside rather than inside the lifecycle Evidence Profile: the Evidence Profile answers what can be known about an Invocation’s acceptance, start, end, failure, or interruption, while the Artifact Result Contract answers whether one exact immutable Artifact can be materialized. Neither answer implies the other, and satisfying the result contract proves capture completeness under that contract—not that the candidate is a semantically complete, correct, useful, or followed Plan.

The exact semantic payload of a v1 Plan Revision is a non-empty ordered sequence of plan text segments in a versioned lossless envelope. Each segment’s decoded text bytes and boundary are preserved exactly; Claude contributes one native plan segment, while Pi contributes every plan-bearing text block selected from one finalized assistant source in order. Neoflow performs no trimming, newline conversion, Unicode normalization, block joining with invented separators, Markdown rewriting, renderer round-trip, or fallback to a lossy “last assistant text” projection. Thinking or reasoning blocks, tool calls and results, retry diagnostics, native file locators, and provider metadata are not Plan content; the result evidence identifies the source shape and extraction rule so those exclusions are explicit rather than silent. Neoflow claims the bytes of its canonical envelope derived from the decoded native values, not unavailable provider wire bytes. An unsupported value, unrepresentable segment, unknown block kind, empty plan, or over-limit source fails the contract; it is never truncated, repaired, or partially materialized.

Each accepted result receives a fresh Artifact Identity independent of Handoff, Invocation, Harness Offer, native message or tool identity, ordinal, filename, and content digest. A digest verifies the exact saved payload but never identifies it. Repeated evidence for the same native source occurrence is idempotent and resolves to the already-created Revision. A new Pi Invocation or Claude Offer creates a distinct Revision occurrence even when its bytes are identical. Pi’s internal retries and tool-loop messages remain within one Invocation and do not create Artifacts of their own; a revised Claude `ExitPlanMode` tool occurrence is a new Offer and therefore, once associated, a new Revision.

The result Durable Commit binds the Revision identity and exact canonical payload to the owning Plan and Workflow, result-contract and Adapter versions, source occurrence identity, content manifest and digest, Attachment and Workspace evidence, correlated Handoff and Invocation or Harness Offer as applicable, decisive completeness evidence, and capture time. Provenance records observed facts, not a claim that the Handoff caused every byte or that the Harness obeyed instructions. Failed durability leaves no Revision and no success projection. Once the commit succeeds, later UI loss, disconnect, decision failure, or restart cannot invalidate the Artifact; recovery reads the same bytes and idempotent evidence.

For Pi Direct `Request Plan Generation`, the v1 result contract is available only in one uninterrupted, exclusively owned Attachment epoch under the frozen reviewed Adapter contract. Neoflow must bind one exact Handoff and Invocation, observe its `agent_start`, detect and reject uncorrelated user or continuation input, retain single-flight ownership through the run, observe `agent_settled`, and at settlement select the unique final persisted assistant entry on the settled session branch. That entry must have `stopReason: "stop"` and an accepted non-empty plan-text shape. The settlement-time stored entry—not streamed deltas, an early `message_end`, `agent_end`, idle observation, or Pi’s text projection—is the source value. Multiple intermediate assistants from tools, compaction, or low-level retry are permitted when they remain inside the correlated envelope; provisional `toolUse` and retry-error entries are provenance, never candidate Revisions. Unexpected follow-ups or any ambiguity about the final source defeat the contract rather than inviting a “last message” guess.

Pi’s finalized source value and `agent_settled` remain separate facts, so the Workspace Writer must durably join them in Neoflow-owned result evidence while both are observable. That evidence binds the Handoff, Invocation and Attachment epoch, selected Pi session-entry occurrence, complete source-block manifest, canonical payload and digest, stop reason, and observed settlement. A connector-owned Pi custom entry may mirror recovery evidence but is not a substitute for Authoritative Workflow State. After bridge loss, session replacement, or process restart, an already-durable Neoflow result commit remains authoritative; a persisted normal-stop Pi assistant without that commit or a verified connector receipt does not prove prior settlement and must never be promoted retroactively.

Pi settlement without a contract-valid source creates no Plan Revision. A `length`, `error`, or `aborted` assistant may preserve exact finalized partial text, but settlement does not make it complete; a tool-terminating run or empty normal stop may likewise end without a Plan result. No-start and proven rejection have no result. Missing settlement, lost source selection, disconnect, replacement, or restart before durable result capture leaves result capture unresolved and cannot be repaired from Pi history alone. An interruption request changes nothing: `aborted` yields no Revision, while a natural `stop` that wins the race may yield one only when every ordinary correlation, settlement, shape, and durability requirement remains intact.

For Claude Code Native Gate, the result source is the one exact bounded `tool_input.plan` value in a validated `PreToolUse:ExitPlanMode` occurrence. The reviewed Adapter must establish the native session, prompt and tool occurrence, exact Attachment and Workspace identities, live gate and deadline, supported decoded text representation, and complete field capture. Its first Durable Commit preserves that exact one-segment candidate and result evidence as part of the Harness Offer before any response. No Claude Stop, Invocation settlement, plan-file read, transcript inspection, content similarity, or prior outbound Handoff is needed or allowed to strengthen it. The Offer may remain unassociated; only explicit Offer Association atomically gives the accepted capture a Plan Revision identity in one selected Planning Workflow. Thus a Revision can exist while the Offer is still undecided, denied, expired, or has unresolved decision delivery, and none of those later lifecycle facts rewrites it.

A duplicate callback for the same Claude native occurrence and bytes is idempotent. Reuse of that occurrence identity with changed bytes or evidence is a contract conflict; a new tool occurrence is a new Offer even when its bytes match. An invalid, malformed, unsupported, empty, oversized, late, identity-incoherent, or incompletely persisted proposal cannot become a Revision or receive an allow. A prior Manual Handoff may remain linked as historical context after association, but it neither correlates nor proves provenance for the Offer; the native occurrence itself is the result source.

The interface therefore presents result evidence independently from Harness lifecycle evidence. `Revision captured` means the exact Artifact commit exists. `No complete revision captured` means decisive evidence disproved the result contract, even if the Invocation ended normally. `Revision capture unknown` means required evidence may have existed but was lost. These projections may coexist honestly with `Ended normally`, `Failed`, `Interrupted`, or `Outcome unknown`; for example, Pi may settle normally without a final plan, while a Claude Revision may be safely captured before any Offer Decision or post-allow Invocation exists. Partial output is never a Revision, never receives Artifact Identity, never satisfies Planning prerequisites, and is never promoted later because a human judges it useful. Minimum diagnostic facts such as source identity, stop reason, byte count, and digest may be retained under existing local evidence policy, but Neoflow does not turn failed output or Harness history into a new transcript Artifact.

A correlated `Request Plan Generation` option may be advertised only when its current Capability and Artifact Result Contract can establish an exact Revision on the supported success path. A route that can dispatch and settle but cannot reliably capture a complete result is explicitly **Plan Revision capture unavailable** for that Handoff; Neoflow must not send it under a `Generate plan` action and hope to scrape the result later. Loss of the contract or its prerequisites before commitment is a blocker, not a Capability Downgrade. Loss after an Invocation begins preserves the real lifecycle evidence and yields `No complete revision captured` or `Revision capture unknown` as warranted; it is not silently retried or retroactively reclassified as Manual Handoff.

The user may instead make a fresh, explicit Manual Handoff for `Request Plan Generation` when its ordinary workflow prerequisites hold. That commitment makes the exact request material ready for the user to carry and claims no Invocation, generated result, delivery, start, settlement, or Artifact. A later valid Claude native Offer remains independently captured and explicitly associated under its own result evidence. Visible Pi or Claude text, clipboard success, a pasted plan, matching bytes, or a human assertion that manual work completed cannot manufacture a generated Plan Revision in v1. After a correlated run ends without a Revision, the user may deliberately start a new generation commitment or choose Manual Handoff while the failed or unresolved evidence remains intact; Neoflow never auto-retries, salvages partial output as a Revision, or treats manual transfer as result recovery.

This decision changes no outbound Handoff payload composition or Planning-completion rule: those remain with [Define v1 Workflow Handoff payload contracts](./WF-031-define-v1-workflow-handoff-payloads.md) and [Define Planning completion and post-approval lifecycle](./WF-032-define-planning-completion-lifecycle.md). No new ticket or fog is required. Canonical language is sharpened in [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md), and the deep Harness Interaction Module Interface is reconciled in [`docs/blueprint/harness-capability-seam.md`](../docs/blueprint/harness-capability-seam.md).
