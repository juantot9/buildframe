## Question

How should Neoflow validate, persist, and associate an attached Claude Code native Plan Harness Offer with a new or existing Planning Workflow—including prior Manual Handoffs, multiple candidate Workflows, a closed or unavailable TUI, duplicate or revised Offers, Attachment or Workspace Identity mismatch, decision deadlines, committed decisions whose delivery is unresolved, and restart—and what atomic relationship among Plan Approval, Feedback or Implementation Handoff, Offer Decision, decision delivery, and any post-allow Invocation preserves conservative start and outcome status when the Native Gate proves no later execution evidence, without inferring conversation ownership or allowing an Offer to inherit unrelated workflow consent?

## Blocked by

- [Test the blueprint for completeness](./WF-017-test-blueprint-completeness.md)
- [Define Manual Handoff commitment and lifecycle](./WF-034-define-manual-handoff-lifecycle.md)

## Resolution comment

Treat every attached Claude Code native Plan callback as one exact, immutable, deadline-bound **Harness Offer** occurrence, not as a Workflow Handoff, Plan Approval, Invocation, or continuation of whichever Planning Workflow happens to be visible. The Workspace Writer validates the reviewed Adapter and contract version, live native gate, native session, prompt and tool occurrence identities, exact Attachment Identity, canonical Workspace Identity on this event, effective route deadline, bounded exact proposal capture, and absence of an identity conflict before any response. The proposal-content digest verifies bytes but never defines Offer identity. Exact Plan Revision completeness and result provenance must satisfy [Define Plan Revision result evidence](./WF-035-define-plan-revision-result-evidence.md); an invalid, unsupported, oversized, ambiguous, late, or workspace-incoherent proposal cannot become a Plan Revision or receive an allow.

The first Durable Commit preserves the exact Offer capture, native identity, Attachment and Workspace evidence, Capability contract basis, deadline and provenance before any external response. An Offer may exist without a Workflow association so a closed TUI, competing candidate Workflows, or a crash cannot force a false owner merely to retain the inbound evidence. If even this commit cannot complete, the present bridge fails closed and may retain bounded diagnostics, but Neoflow has no authoritative Offer or Artifact to claim.

An initial or otherwise uncorrelated Offer always requires an explicit **Offer Association**: the user selects one eligible existing Planning Workflow or creates a new one, and the association materializes the accepted capture as a new immutable Plan Revision owned by that Workflow. Creating the Workflow also establishes its exact harness-neutral Planning Origin under [Define native-offer Planning origin semantics](./WF-037-define-native-offer-planning-origin.md). Even one matching candidate is a suggested choice, not automatic ownership. The TUI may combine the choice with the user’s next semantic action, but the durable association remains distinct from Approval, Feedback, Handoff, and the Offer Decision. Once committed, it is immutable; the Revision is not moved between Workflows to repair a mistaken choice.

Workspace and Attachment facts constrain and explain candidates rather than choosing among them. An existing Workflow from another Workspace Identity is ineligible; path aliases may canonicalize to the same checkout, but a linked worktree or separate clone does not. A callback whose session or checkout no longer matches its Attachment Identity fails closed rather than attaching to that Workflow or another current session. A same-workspace Workflow associated with a different prior session may be selected only explicitly, with the destination change and any known overlapping activity visible; Neoflow still claims no conversation transfer. Cancelled, completed, trashed, or quarantined Workflows do not silently reopen or absorb an Offer—their independent reopening or recovery rules must first make them eligible.

A prior Manual Handoff may appear as useful candidate evidence when its declared Harness, workspace, session label, exact Plan material, or timing resembles the Offer. It never correlates the Offer, proves the user carried the material, ranks one candidate as authoritative, creates an Invocation retroactively, or contributes consent. After explicit association, the Offer may reference that Manual Handoff as historical context while external use remains unverified. Matching proposal bytes, native plan filename, model, timing, focused TUI state, or a single open Workflow have the same non-authoritative status.

Multiple eligible Workflows therefore produce one explicit picker with `Create new Planning Workflow`; Neoflow never uses “most recent” as a tie-breaker. Multiple distinct Offers remain independently actionable by exact human-readable destination and proposal identity. A duplicate callback for the same native occurrence and exact bytes is idempotent and resolves to its existing Offer; reuse of that native identity with different bytes or workspace evidence is a contract conflict that fails closed and enters diagnostics or Recovery Quarantine rather than rewriting history. A new tool occurrence is a new Offer and Plan Revision even when its bytes are identical.

One narrow revision relationship is strong enough to retain Workflow association without another picker: a reviewed Adapter must prove the same Attachment Identity and prompt occurrence, a prior exact deny was delivered to that gate, and the new tool occurrence is the gate’s subsequent revision attempt. That successor gets new Offer and Plan Revision identities and joins the same Plan, but inherits no Approval, Annotation disposition, Feedback Batch, Handoff, Offer Decision, or consent. If prior deny delivery is unresolved, the prompt changed, the gate closed, allow was delivered, or any identity fact differs, the new Offer is uncorrelated and requires explicit association again. Content similarity and timing never repair missing native causality.

A reachable Workspace Writer handles intake independently of any TUI client. A closed or unavailable TUI leaves the valid Offer durably awaiting user attention while the bridge holds only for its disclosed protocol window; it never auto-selects a Workflow, auto-approves, or reuses consent. If no valid user decision commits before the serialized deadline, the Offer expires undecided and the bridge emits a fail-closed **Native Gate Refusal** where it still can. That refusal proves only that this Neoflow gate did not authorize the proposal: it is not an Offer Decision, user rejection, Feedback, Plan Approval, or Harness failure. If the bridge or Writer is absent before intake, ordinary Claude behavior can be constrained only to the extent the installed bridge actually fails closed; Neoflow must not claim an Offer it never durably received.

Deadline expiry, association, cancellation, and decision commands serialize through the Workspace Writer. If expiry commits first, no later decision is accepted. If a valid decision commits first, that immutable decision wins even if delivery subsequently misses the deadline; Neoflow does not replace it with a synthetic deny. Workflow Cancellation before decision prevents a cancelled Workflow from supplying authority and may cause only a Native Gate Refusal. Cancellation after decision preserves the decision, Handoffs, receipts, delivery evidence and any Invocation and cannot revoke a response that may have escaped.

Plan Approval remains the already-defined separate local decision over one exact associated Plan Revision. It neither decides nor allows the Offer, and the native Claude label “User approved Claude’s plan” must never be treated as independent consent. Approval must be established by its own explicit action before implementation commitment; the interface may lead directly to the next focused action without collapsing the two decisions. Approval Withdrawal and `Begin implementation` serialize: withdrawal first blocks the Handoff, while Handoff commitment first consumes the Approval’s active eligibility under the Planning lifecycle.

The semantic paths from a live associated Offer are:

- `Request revision` atomically commits the exact Feedback Batch, its Native Gate Workflow Handoff and Commitment Receipt, and a deny Offer Decision before attempting delivery. The decision cannot refer to draft or later-edited feedback. An explicit user deny with no Feedback may commit an Offer Decision alone and makes no claim that feedback was supplied.
- `Begin implementation` requires the exact active Plan Approval and every existing Implementation Handoff prerequisite. One Durable Commit establishes the exact Implementation Handoff, Commitment Receipt, allow Offer Decision, and one post-allow Invocation bound to this Handoff, Offer and Attachment before delivery is attempted. A bare allow, an Approval without a Handoff, or a Handoff relying on another Offer or Revision is blocked.
- A Native Gate Refusal caused by deadline, invalidity, missing authority, or unavailable infrastructure creates none of those semantic records.

Competing feedback, allow, withdrawal, cancellation, and duplicate UI commands are serialized. An exact duplicate command is idempotent; after one Offer Decision commits, a different decision is rejected rather than appended as a correction. Failed durability leaves the prior state unchanged and emits no semantic response. The exact Handoff payload remains governed by [Define v1 Workflow Handoff payload contracts](./WF-031-define-v1-workflow-handoff-payloads.md); the Adapter may encode only that committed structure and minimum correlation identity, never ambient conversation or current workspace content.

**Offer Decision Delivery** is a separate append-only fact: pending, proven delivered to the exact live gate, proven not delivered, or unresolved. Delivered deny proves only that the exact gate response reached Claude, not that Claude understood or addressed Feedback; a later correlated Offer is separate evidence. Delivered allow proves only route acceptance, not that implementation started, changed a file, ended, or followed the Plan. Socket acceptance, bridge intent, Plan-mode UI, partial output, idle state, model labels, and an unacknowledged callback are not stronger evidence.

A repeated callback may return the existing committed decision only when it proves the same still-live native occurrence and unexpired deadline; this is idempotent completion of the same delivery, not replay. A new Offer, even with identical content, never receives an old response. When a decision was committed but exact delivery cannot be established before disconnect, gate closure, deadline, or restart, its delivery remains unresolved forever unless the same exact live occurrence supplies the missing evidence. The Decision, Approval, Feedback Batch, Handoff, receipt, and Revision remain intact; none is rolled back or silently reissued.

The allow composite creates its Invocation in `awaiting start` because Neoflow has authorized one exact post-gate harness continuation but has not observed execution. Proof that allow was not delivered may transition it to `rejected` / **Did not start**. Ambiguous delivery makes it `unresolved`. Even proven allow delivery does not establish start, and Claude’s v1 Native Gate Capability supplies no trustworthy post-allow start or Terminal Evidence; once that exact gate closes without such evidence, the Invocation becomes `unresolved`, never `active`, `ended normally`, `failed`, or `interrupted` by inference. This expected evidence limitation is disclosed at commitment, preserves duplicate-execution warnings for later work, and cannot be cleared by Plan Approval, Workflow completion, workspace changes, or a later Review.

Restart reconstructs only durable records. An undecided Offer may resume as live only if the reviewed Adapter re-presents the exact native occurrence and proves the same Attachment, Workspace, payload, contract and unexpired gate still waits; Neoflow never probes Claude’s plan file, transcript, or session store to infer this. Otherwise it expires undecided. A committed decision with no durable delivery fact becomes unresolved unless that same live occurrence returns; an allow Invocation follows the conservative rejected-versus-unresolved rules above. No decision, Feedback, Handoff, or Invocation is automatically resent, redirected, recreated, or attached to the next Offer, and corrupt records are quarantined rather than reconstructed from Harness state.

These rules preserve Harness conversation ownership: Neoflow owns the captured Offer, explicit Workflow relationship, Plan Revision, human decisions, Handoffs, receipts, and evidence; Claude owns the surrounding conversation and execution. Canonical terms are sharpened in [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md), and the Harness Interaction Module summary is reconciled in [`docs/blueprint/harness-capability-seam.md`](../docs/blueprint/harness-capability-seam.md). No new ticket or fog is required: result completeness, exact Handoff payloads, Planning completion, and cross-session continuation remain in their existing tickets.
